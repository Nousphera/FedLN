NNC · before training
A frozen encoder finds other local examples with similar image features. A vote among their stored labels corrects the sample’s label.
AKD · during training
For clients with detected noise, an embedding teacher guides local features alongside the original-label signal. The labels stay unchanged.
NA-FedAvg · at the server
After ordinary warm-up, clients send global- and local-model confidence scores once. The server estimates each client’s noise and reuses it to weight later updates by data share times estimated reliability.

Label patterns, pretrained representations and available computation determine which route is useful.

Abstract

Federated Learning (FL) is a distributed machine learning paradigm that enables learning models from decentralized private datasets where the labeling effort is entrusted to the clients. While most existing FL approaches assume high-quality labels are readily available on users’ devices, in reality, label noise can naturally occur in FL and is closely related to clients’ characteristics. Due to scarcity of available data and significant label noise variations among clients in FL, existing state-of-the-art centralized approaches exhibit unsatisfactory performance, whereas prior FL studies rely on excessive on-device computational schemes or additional clean data available on the server. We propose FedLN, a framework to deal with label noise across different FL training stages, namely FL initialization, on-device model training, and server model aggregation, able to accommodate the diverse computational capabilities of devices in an FL system. Specifically, FedLN computes per-client noise level estimation in a single federated round and improves the models’ performance by either correcting or mitigating the effect of noisy samples. Our evaluation on various publicly available vision and audio datasets demonstrates a 22% improvement on average compared to other existing methods for a label noise level of 60%. We further validate the efficiency of FedLN in human-annotated real-world noisy datasets and report a 4.8% increase on average in models’ recognition performance, highlighting that FedLN can be useful for improving FL services provided to everyday users.

The method

How it works

NNC

Nearest Neighbor-based Correction

At initialization · correct the labels

A pretrained encoder maps each client’s examples into an embedding space. Nearby examples vote on the label. When that vote disagrees with the stored label, NNC replaces the label before federated training.

Examples and embeddings stay fixed. The fraction of disagreements also supplies a local noise estimate.

y~i= arg maxc ∑j∈Nk(g(xi)) 1[yj=c]

Count one vote per neighbor; keep the most frequent class.

g
Pretrained feature encoder.
Nk
Indices of the k nearest other local embeddings by Euclidean distance.
yj
The neighbor’s observed label; tied votes are broken randomly.

Useful embeddings help, but a neighborhood dominated by the wrong class can still vote incorrectly.

Noise estimation and source

The encoder is used locally during initialization. The per-client noise estimate is the number of vote–label mismatches divided by the client’s sample count. The displayed voting rule is a compact restatement, with repeated labels counted separately.

AKD

Adaptive Knowledge Distillation

During local training · add teacher guidance

AKD keeps the observed labels and adds a teacher loss for clients with detected label noise. The teacher supplies pretrained embeddings or predictions from the aggregated global model.

The local model learns from its labels and this additional supervision in one combined objective.

LAKD=LCE+βuε(nlm)LKD

Add teacher guidance when the client’s noise estimate activates it.

CE / KD
Cross-entropy label loss / knowledge-distillation teacher loss.
nlm, uε
Client m’s estimated noise and its threshold-based gate.
β
Controls the teacher loss’s contribution.

The teacher is extra supervision; it does not force a potentially incorrect replacement label.

Teacher losses and source

Embedding supervision uses mean absolute error. Global-model supervision uses Kullback–Leibler divergence between temperature-scaled outputs. The reported results on this page use embedding supervision.

NA-FedAvg

Noise-aware Federated Averaging

At server aggregation · reweight client updates

After a warm-up period, clients compute energy scores using their local and global models. The server uses the scores to estimate each client’s label-noise level once.

Later rounds reuse those estimates: a client’s data share is multiplied by its estimated clean-label fraction.

γm=(1−nlm)NmN

Noisier clients contribute less to the shared model.

γm
Client m’s aggregation weight.
Nm / N
Its share of all training samples.
1 − nlm
Its estimated fraction of clean labels.

This avoids a separate pretrained encoder, but still depends on distinguishing relatively reliable clients.

One-time estimation and source

Energy scores come from the model’s output logits. Score computation and communication occur in the designated estimation round; subsequent rounds continue local training and noise-aware aggregation. The equation reproduces the paper’s weight without adding a normalization term.

What changes between the columns?

Noise sparsity changes from 0% to 100% while the corruption rate stays at 70%. Random noise spreads wrong labels across classes; class flipping concentrates them. Takeaways

Match the intervention to the noise.

  • NNC

    Neighborhood quality matters. Strong gains under random noise; incorrect majorities can hurt under class flipping.

  • AKD

    Guide learning without replacing labels. Embedding-based AKD gives the highest CIFAR-100N accuracy among the compared methods.

  • NA-FedAvg

    Reduce noisier clients’ influence. Reweight updates when client reliability can be distinguished.
