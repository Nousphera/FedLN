Labeling Chaos to Learning Harmony: Federated Learning with Noisy Labels

Vasileios Tsouvalas, Aaqib Saeed, Tanir Ozcelebi, Nirvana Meratnia

Eindhoven University of Technology

FedLN estimates client label noise and addresses it through initialization, local training, or server aggregation.

Abstract

FedLN studies learning from noisy labels in federated systems, where data remain on clients and labeling quality varies between devices. Its three methods use client noise estimates for nearest-neighbor label correction, adaptive knowledge distillation, or noise-aware model aggregation. The journal paper evaluates these approaches on vision and audio tasks; the public implementation includes CIFAR-10 and EuroSAT experiment entry points.

Label Noise Estimation in Federated Setting

In federated setting, the label noise is directly affected by discrepancies in clients' labeling systems and users' expertize. These discrepancies result in varying label noise profiles across clients. With FedLN, we provide two simple yet effective approaches for estimating a per-client label noise level and correctly identifying any client holding high-quality data.

  • Embedding-based Discovery of Noisy Label
  • Model Confidence as a Proxy for Label Noise

Federated Learning under Presence of Label Noise

Under a federated learning regime, clients' local data follow a non-i.i.d. distribution both in terms of data samples and noise profiles, while no global entity can directly access the complete pool of data, rendering data-centric and model decision-based centralized approaches infeasible or ineffective in FL. In FedLN, we exploit per-client noise level estimates to mitigate the effect of noisy labels on the federated model's performance.
